### Nature's Solution to Marine Biofouling: What is Cretenyne B?

Many natural products produced by marine organisms possess unique properties that can enrich our lives. For instance, "biofouling," the attachment of barnacles and algae to ship hulls, is a significant problem that reduces fuel efficiency and harms the environment. Natural substances are garnering attention as potential "antifouling agents" to combat this issue.
Cretenyne B, the subject of this research, is one such natural product. It holds complex molecular structures and the potential to prevent marine fouling.

### The Stereochemical Puzzle: Deciphering Complex Molecular Shapes

While the relative configuration of a part of cretenyne B's molecular structure—specifically, the tetrahydrofuran moiety—had been determined in previous studies, the precise three-dimensional shapes (stereochemistries) at the C6 and C13 positions of its side chain, which are "chiral centers" (atoms with a non-superimposable mirror image, like left and right hands), remained unresolved. Since the orientation of these connections significantly alters the molecule's properties, solving this "stereochemical puzzle" was a crucial challenge.

### The Blueprint for Molecules: Challenging Total Synthesis

To predict the C6 stereochemistry of cretenyne B, we first compared the NMR (Nuclear Magnetic Resonance) data of natural cretenyne B with that of C6 stereoisomers (molecules differing only in the C6 configuration) of cretenyne A, a related compound we had previously synthesized. This analysis helped narrow down the possible candidates for natural cretenyne B.

Next, we embarked on the "total synthesis" of two candidate diastereomers, 2a and 2b, building them from scratch. We meticulously constructed the complex molecular "blueprint" using precise chemical reactions. Key transformations included "5-exo-tet-cyclization" to form a specific ring, "cis-selective olefinations" to create double bonds in a particular geometry, and "stereoselective chlorination" to introduce a chlorine atom in a specific orientation. Towards the late stage of the synthesis, we successfully employed "Luche reduction" as a branching step to efficiently produce both candidate molecules.

### Solving the Mystery and New Discoveries: Antifouling Potential

By comparing the properties of the synthesized candidate molecules (2a and 2b) with those of natural cretenyne B, it was revealed that natural (-)-cretenyne B is the enantiomer (a molecule that is a non-superimposable mirror image, like left and right hands) of the synthesized 2a. This resolved the long-standing stereochemical puzzle.

Furthermore, remarkably, both synthesized 2a and 2b were found to exhibit "antifouling activity" against the cypris larvae of barnacles. Importantly, these molecules were also confirmed to be non-toxic to the larvae, hinting at their potential for developing new, environmentally friendly antifouling agents. The synthetic intermediates obtained in this work are also expected to be applicable to the synthesis of other related natural products, such as cretenynes C-E.

### The Excitement of Organic Synthesis

This research exemplifies the thrill of "organic synthesis," where complex natural product structures, whose shapes are unknown, are elucidated like solving a puzzle, and then precisely constructed. Controlling the connections and spatial arrangement of individual atoms to create new functional materials is truly a "molecular craftsmanship." It's a field full of promising discoveries that can enrich our lives.
