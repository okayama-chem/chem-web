### What Happens at the "Border" Between Solids and Liquids?

We know that some substances, like oil and water, don't mix. But at the "border" where a solid meets a liquid—an "interface"—invisible yet incredibly important interactions are constantly taking place. For example, inside the batteries used in smartphones and electric cars, electricity is generated as electrons move back and forth between the solid electrodes and the liquid electrolyte. Similarly, within our bodies, various chemical substances are exchanged as information across cell membranes, which are also a type of solid-liquid interface.

### Electrochemistry: Measuring "Information Transfer"

The field of "electrochemistry" studies these "information transfers" occurring at solid-liquid interfaces by examining electrical properties, such as electron movement. Sensors that detect specific substances, or fuel cells that generate electricity, both utilize mechanisms of electrochemical information transfer. However, reactions at these interfaces are highly complex and can behave differently depending on the material and environment, making it challenging to understand each phenomenon individually.

### Discovering a "Universal Rule" Applicable to All Materials

In this research, a "universal rule" (scaling law) was identified for electrochemical "information transfer" at solid-liquid interfaces. This rule applies commonly, regardless of the specific material or situation. This means discovering a simple and elegant law hidden beneath seemingly complex phenomena, much like a universal law of physics that applies anywhere in the universe. Understanding such a universal rule allows for more efficient comprehension and prediction of phenomena occurring with various materials and under diverse environmental conditions.

### Materials Chemistry: Shaping the Future

The discovery of this "universal rule" has the potential to aid in the development of new high-performance sensors and the design of more efficient energy conversion devices (such as batteries and fuel cells). Universal knowledge, independent of specific materials, provides a powerful foundation for future technological development. "Materials Chemistry," which explores the fundamental properties of substances and how they interact, is an exciting field that generates new materials and technologies to enrich our lives.
