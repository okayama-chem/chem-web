### The Unseen World of Ions in Aqueous Solutions

Water is essential for life and countless chemical processes. Dissolved "ions" in water play critical roles in everything from biological functions and chemical reactions to industrial applications. For example, the saltiness of sweat comes from sodium and chloride ions, and batteries operate through the movement of ions.
However, accurately understanding and predicting how individual ions interact with water molecules and "occupy space" within an aqueous solution has been a long-standing challenge.

### Measuring the "Space" of an Ion: What is Partial Molar Volume?

One crucial property of an ion dissolved in water is its "partial molar volume." Simply put, this refers to the effective "volume" or "space" an individual ion takes up within an aqueous solution. This "volume" is not just related to macroscopic properties like the solution's density or viscosity, but also deeply influences chemical behaviors, such as how ions react with other molecules or accumulate at interfaces.
Historically, precisely measuring or theoretically predicting the partial molar volume of individual ions has been difficult.

### Introducing the iPMV Force Field: A Novel Computational Model

To address this challenge, our research group developed a new computational model called the "iPMV (ion Partial Molar Volume) force field." A force field is essentially a "rulebook" that defines how atoms and molecules interact with each other in a simulation. The iPMV force field is specifically designed to accurately reproduce the experimental "individual ion partial molar volumes" (obtained through a specialized technique called the ultrasonic vibrational potential method) for a wide range of common ions, including Li+, Na+, K+, Cs+, Mg2+, Ca2+, F-, Cl-, Br-, I-, OH-, and H3O+.
A key innovation in our model is the use of a "charge scaling factor" (0.75), which subtly adjusts the ionic charge to more realistically represent ion-water interactions.

### Exceptional Accuracy from a Single Model

What makes the iPMV force field remarkable is its ability to consistently predict a wide array of aqueous solution properties with high accuracy, despite its straightforward design. For example, a single set of iPMV parameters can explain and predict volumetric properties, interfacial behavior (how ions behave at the water-air boundary), salting-out/in effects (how ion presence affects solubility), and even transport coefficients (how easily ions move through the solution).
This robust performance suggests a fundamental correlation: the partial molar volumes of individual ions are deeply connected to the broader physical and chemical properties of aqueous electrolyte solutions. This new computational model is expected to have broad applications in fields such as battery material development, drug design, and predicting the behavior of pollutants in the environment.
