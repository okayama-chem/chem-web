### The Invisible Information Exchange at Solid-Liquid Interfaces

At the "interface" where solids and liquids meet, various chemical reactions and energy conversion processes take place. However, it has been unclear how deeply the "electrochemical potential" (an electrical force) at these interfaces influences the solid. It was like a puzzle: how far can information reach beyond an invisible wall?

### Unveiling the "Magic Number" $u$ for Information Transfer

Our research discovered a single "dimensionless factor $u$" that determines how far this "electrochemical information" propagates into the solid. This factor $u$ is defined as the ratio of the effective electrostatic separation between the liquid and the probe location to the electrostatic propagation length within the solid. We demonstrated that using $u$, the information transfer follows a universal inverse-square scaling law, meaning its strength diminishes in proportion to the square of the distance. This is a powerful finding, as it is independent of the microscopic details of the material.

### A New Blueprint for Subsurface Sensing

This law reveals that "electrostatic screening," a phenomenon that impedes information propagation into the solid, is a fundamental constraint on information transfer. In essence, it allows us to quantitatively predict how information travels. This discovery provides crucial "design principles" for developing new technologies, such as "subsurface electrochemical sensors," which can detect impurities or changes within a solid from its surface using electrical methods.

### Opening New Horizons in Interface Science

This study demonstrates that complex phenomena at solid-liquid interfaces can be understood through simple mathematical laws. Unraveling the flow of invisible electrochemical information has the potential to lead to a wide range of applications, including the development of new materials, improvements in energy conversion efficiency, and sensor technologies in the medical field.
