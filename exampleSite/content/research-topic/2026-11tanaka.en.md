### What are Thio- and Selenoesters, and Why are They Important?

You might not be familiar with the terms “thioester” and “selenoester,” but these compounds are incredibly important in creating pharmaceuticals and advanced materials, known as functional materials. For instance, they can be key components in medicines or serve as building blocks for new plastics and electronic materials with unique properties. Traditional methods for synthesizing thio- and selenoesters often require special chemical reagents called “activating agents” to kickstart the reaction. These methods can sometimes be cumbersome or have a greater environmental impact.

### Harnessing Light for Chemical Transformations

In recent years, “photochemical reactions”—chemical processes driven by light, such as sunlight or LED light—have attracted significant attention. They are considered an environmentally friendly and efficient way to conduct chemical synthesis. While there have been some studies on using light to convert aldehydes into thioesters, most of these relied on “photocatalysts” (substances that accelerate reactions with light without being consumed). Furthermore, the photochemical synthesis of selenoesters from aldehydes has been largely unreported until now.

Our research group tackled this challenge and developed a new method that uses only purple-LED irradiation to efficiently synthesize both thio- and selenoesters directly from aldehydes.

### The Key: C–H Bromination and Acyl-Bromide Intermediates

The secret to this new synthetic method lies in a reaction called “C–H bromination” and the “acyl-bromide” intermediate that forms during the process. First, the C–H bond (the bond between a carbon and a hydrogen atom) of an aldehyde is brominated using light. This forms an acyl-bromide, which acts like a chemical “bridge.” This acyl-bromide then readily reacts with other compounds, such as thiols or selenophenol, to form the desired thio- and selenoesters.

This method allows for the synthesis of a wide variety of thio- and selenoesters. Compared to conventional approaches, which typically start from carboxylic acids or acyl chlorides and require activating agents, our method proceeds under milder conditions. This makes it a valuable alternative for the synthesis of pharmaceuticals and functional materials.

### Organic Chemistry: Designing Materials for the Future

This research opens up new avenues for synthesizing important pharmaceutical and advanced materials by utilizing light as a clean energy source. Organic chemistry is a fundamental science that creates a vast array of substances around us, enriching our lives. Designing and synthesizing new molecules that were previously challenging, all with the power of light—that's the excitement of organic chemistry.
