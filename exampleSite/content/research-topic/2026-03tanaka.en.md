### What are Helicenes? Discovering Spiral-Shaped Molecules

In the world of chemistry, catalysts are like magical substances that help create various materials around us. Today, we introduce a new type of catalyst called "helicene," a molecule with a unique spiral shape. As its name suggests, helicenes are known for their twisted, staircase-like structure, and their special form holds promise for various functions.

In this research, a new type of "cationic [4]-helicene" (a helicene molecule with a positive charge) was synthesized, where sulfur and oxygen atoms bridge the molecular backbone. This charge influences its performance as a catalyst.

### Red Light Powers Chemical Reactions: The Potential of Photoredox Catalysis

The helicene synthesized in this study was found to function as a "photoredox catalyst." A photoredox catalyst is a substance that uses light energy to facilitate the transfer of electrons (redox reactions) between molecules, thereby promoting chemical reactions.

Often, chemical reactions require heat energy or strong ultraviolet light. However, this helicene catalyst can efficiently drive chemical reactions using "red LED light," a common and relatively low-energy light source found in our daily lives. Using red light offers advantages such as being more environmentally friendly and potentially enabling selective reactions.

### New Synthetic Methods Pave the Way for Future Catalysts

This novel helicene was created using a clever synthetic approach called "Friedel–Crafts-S<sub>N</sub>Ar tandem reactions," which involves performing multiple chemical reactions in sequence. Specifically, by combining compounds known as "diaryl sulfide" or "diaryl ether" with "(thio)salicylic acid," we successfully produced the desired spiral-shaped molecules efficiently.

This research not only develops a catalyst with a novel molecular structure but also demonstrates an efficient new method for its synthesis. This opens up possibilities for developing environmentally friendly chemical reactions and precise synthesis in fields like pharmaceuticals.
