### What is CO2 Hydrate and Why is it a Problem for Transportation?

As a measure against global warming, technologies for capturing carbon dioxide (CO2) from factories and transporting or storing it in liquid form underground are gaining attention. However, a troublesome issue can arise in pipelines transporting this liquid CO2: the formation of "CO2 hydrate" due to even tiny amounts of mixed water.

A "hydrate" is a substance where water molecules form cage-like structures, trapping gas molecules (in this case, CO2) inside. Since it's a solid crystal, much like ice, there's a risk of pipeline blockages. To achieve safe and efficient CO2 transportation, it's crucial to understand the conditions under which CO2 hydrate formation can be avoided.

### How Much Water Can Liquid CO2 Hold? The Importance of Solubility

This research investigated how much water can dissolve in liquid CO2, also known as its "solubility." Since too much dissolved water can lead to hydrate formation, knowing the "safe amount of water" that doesn't cause hydrate to form is extremely important.

Our research team evaluated the solubility of water under various conditions, such as when liquid CO2 coexists with CO2 hydrate or with liquid water. By doing so, we aimed to clarify the temperature and pressure conditions under which CO2 can be transported without hydrate formation.

### Elucidating Thermodynamic Conditions to Avoid Hydrate Formation

The main objective of this study was to identify the "thermodynamic conditions" to avoid CO2 hydrate formation during liquid CO2 transportation processes. Thermodynamic conditions refer to the specific temperature, pressure, and other factors that determine the stable state (liquid, solid, gas, etc.) of a substance.

We used computational chemistry to investigate these conditions in detail. By clarifying how much water can dissolve in liquid CO2 and under what circumstances hydrate begins to form, this research provides crucial guidelines for preventing hydrate-related problems in CO2 transportation operations.

### Theoretical Chemistry Supporting Future CO2 Transportation

This study supports technologies contributing to the global challenge of CO2 emission reduction from a fundamental chemical perspective. By unraveling the invisible world of molecules through theory and computation, we can find clues to solve complex societal problems.
