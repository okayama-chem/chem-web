### What Happens at the Boundary Between Solids and Liquids?

In our daily lives, solids and liquids meet in countless places. For example, inside a battery, electrodes (solids) and electrolyte solutions (liquids) come into contact, and electricity flows through chemical reactions. The place where a solid and a liquid meet is called a "solid-liquid interface," and the chemical reactions occurring there are called "electrochemistry." At this interface, various kinds of "information" are exchanged through electrical signals and the movement of substances.

### Uncovering "Universal Rules" Governing Information Transfer

The theme of this research is to explore how "electrochemical information" is transferred at solid-liquid interfaces, and whether there are "universal scaling laws" (rules that hold true regardless of scale) that govern this process.
"Universal" means applicable broadly, not limited to specific substances or conditions. A "scaling law" describes how properties change in a consistent way even when the size or scale of something changes. We hypothesize that there might be hidden, simple rules governing the complex flow of information at these interfaces.

### Why is Finding "Universal Rules" Important?

If universal scaling laws are found for electrochemical information transfer at solid-liquid interfaces, it would be a significant discovery.
Understanding these laws could contribute to the development of various technologies, such as new high-performance batteries, sensors, and catalysts (substances that speed up chemical reactions). Unraveling how materials behave and transfer information at the nanoscale (one-billionth of a meter) is key to deepening fundamental science and paving the way for future technologies.
