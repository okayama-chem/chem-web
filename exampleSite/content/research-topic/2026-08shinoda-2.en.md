### The Cellular Delivery Vehicle: What are Lipid Nanoparticles (LNPs)?

When we fall ill, medicines need to reach the specific cells causing the disease in our bodies. However, simply administering drugs often makes it difficult for them to efficiently reach target cells or enter inside them. This is where "Lipid Nanoparticles (LNPs)" come into play. LNPs are tiny, capsule-like substances made of lipid membranes. They encapsulate nucleic acids (like gene therapies and mRNA vaccines) and act as "delivery vehicles" to transport them to target cells and release them inside, revolutionizing modern medical technology.

However, after LNPs enter a cell, the mechanism by which they efficiently release their encapsulated cargo into the cytoplasm (the main part of the cell)—specifically the process called "endosomal escape," which involves escaping from internal cellular compartments (endosomes)—has not been fully understood.

### Unraveling Membrane Penetration with Molecular Simulations

In this study, we used "molecular simulations" to investigate the molecular-level mechanism of how LNPs fuse with the endosomal membrane of cells and release their nucleic acid payloads. Molecular simulation is a technique that calculates the movements and interactions of molecules on a computer, allowing us to predict and analyze the behavior of substances at a very small scale, much like looking through a microscope.

Our simulations revealed that LNPs primarily follow a "stalk-pore mechanism" when fusing with the endosomal membrane. This mechanism involves the initial formation of a thin "stalk-like" structure between the LNP and the membrane, which then expands to create a "pore," allowing the contents to be released. Furthermore, we were able to reproduce and explain key experimental observations through simulation, such as the transfer of "ionizable lipids" (a component of LNPs) to the endosomal membrane, which reorients nucleic acids and facilitates the formation and expansion of the stalk and pore.

### Key Molecular Determinants for Efficient Drug Delivery

This research also identified several critical molecular-level factors that significantly influence the efficiency of LNP endosomal escape. Specifically, we found that the "lipid shape" of the LNP, its "pH sensitivity" (how its properties change with acidity), the "membrane tension" on the cell membrane, and the "nucleic acid encapsulation state" are all key to efficiently delivering drugs into the cytoplasm.

These findings not only deepen our understanding of how LNPs function within cells but also provide new design principles for developing higher-performing and more efficient gene therapies and RNA vaccines.
