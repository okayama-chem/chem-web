### Supercooled Electrolytes: A New Frontier for Li-ion Batteries

Lithium-ion batteries are indispensable for our smartphones and electric vehicles. To create even higher-performance batteries, advancements in materials that efficiently transport lithium ions (Li+), known as "electrolytes," are crucial.

This research focuses on a special class of materials called "deeply supercooled solvent-free electrolytes." These materials contain no solvent and are highly viscous liquids that remain in a "supercooled" state—meaning they don't crystallize and solidify even at low temperatures. In such electrolytes, Li+ ions are reported to move almost exclusively (with a "transference number" approaching unity), a phenomenon that is usually uncommon. However, the detailed microscopic mechanism of this movement has remained a mystery.

### Li-ion Movement: A "Hopping" Mechanism

We approached this mystery using a powerful tool in chemistry: "molecular dynamics simulations." This computational method tracks how atoms and molecules move over time, allowing us to understand the properties of materials from a microscopic perspective.

Our study investigated lithium (fluorosulfonyl)(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)amide, Li[FTA], and its "polymer-in-salt electrolytes"—where Li[FTA] is mixed with poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), a large molecule formed by many repeating units. The simulations revealed that Li+ ions exhibit translational mobility nearly an order of magnitude higher than that of the surrounding FTA– anions. Crucially, their movement was characterized by "hopping" – an intermittent and non-Gaussian displacement, meaning the ions temporarily reside at one site and then abruptly jump to another.

### Heterogeneous Dynamics: The Key to Fast Transport

Further detailed analysis showed that the dynamics within the electrolyte are "spatially heterogeneous," meaning Li+ ions move differently depending on their location. Specifically, Li+ motion is suppressed near the polymer interface but significantly enhanced in regions where the polymer concentration is lower.

In polymer-dilute regions, the coordination (the attractive interaction) between Li+ ions and anions is weakened. This makes it easier for Li+ ions to detach from anions, leading to more frequent hopping events. The combination of this "hopping" mechanism and the "spatially heterogeneous dynamics" reveals the secret behind the efficient transport of lithium ions.

### Guiding the Design of Next-Generation Battery Materials

This research provides crucial insights for developing high-performance lithium-ion batteries. For designing solvent-free electrolytes that are safer and can efficiently transport only lithium ions (high transference number electrolytes), understanding how to create an environment conducive to frequent Li+ hopping is paramount.

Utilizing computational simulations to unravel the movements in the invisible world of atoms and molecules, and thereby contributing to the development of new materials, is what makes theoretical and computational chemistry so exciting. This field holds immense potential to contribute significantly to the development of high-performance batteries that will help solve future energy challenges.
