### The Invisible Journey of Molecules in Solid Materials
Our daily lives are surrounded by various solid materials, such as polymers like plastics and inorganic solids like ceramics. These materials often absorb small molecules, like water vapor or gases, and allow them to slowly move through their interior (diffusion). For instance, the ability of food packaging plastic to prevent moisture, or a fuel cell membrane to selectively pass certain gases, is determined by these mechanisms of "molecular absorption and diffusion."

However, it has been extremely challenging to observe in detail how these invisibly small molecules move around inside complex solid materials.

### Efficiently Mapping Molecular Pathways with 3D Free Energy Maps
In this study, we developed a new, efficient computational method to create a "free energy map" for small molecules diffusing within solid media. Free energy is an indicator of a molecule's stability at a given location, and like a geographical map, it shows "stable regions (valleys)" and "unstable regions (hills)." With such a map, we can understand the "pathways" — where molecules are likely to absorb and where they face resistance in movement.

Previous methods for creating these maps required immense computational time. We significantly reduced the computational cost by incorporating novel concepts called "importance sampling" and "high-temperature sampling." This allowed us to quickly generate highly detailed 3D free energy maps, comprising over a million data points with a resolution of just 0.05 nanometers (the size of a few atoms).

### Revealing Water Absorption in Common Plastics
We applied our developed method to study the absorption and diffusion of water molecules in common plastic materials like polylactic acid and polypropylene. The results showed excellent agreement between our 3D free energy maps and values obtained from conventional calculation methods, as well as experimental water absorption rates. This confirms the reliability of our computational approach.

The maps provide detailed, molecular-level information, such as the spatial distribution of absorption sites (where water molecules prefer to reside) and the 3D diffusion pathway networks (how molecules move from one absorption site to another). By identifying "saddle points" (energetic bottlenecks) along these pathways, we can pinpoint the critical regions that limit diffusion.

### Towards Rational Material Design
This research provides a powerful tool for a deeper, molecular-level understanding of absorption and diffusion mechanisms in materials. By revealing the molecular structures of absorption sites, their free energies, and the specific pathways of molecular movement, we can gain crucial information for designing new materials with tailored properties. For example, this could lead to the development of "plastics that are more resistant to water permeation" or "membranes that efficiently separate specific gases."

By leveraging the power of computer simulation and theoretical computational chemistry to unravel the behavior of invisible molecules, we aim to contribute to the development of future materials. This is the goal of our research.
