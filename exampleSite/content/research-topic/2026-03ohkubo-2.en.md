### The Silent Threat to Buildings: Carbonation Shrinkage in Cement

Cement is a vital material that supports our infrastructure, from buildings to bridges. However, when hardened cement paste reacts with carbon dioxide (CO2) in the air, it can gradually shrink and develop cracks. This phenomenon, known as "carbonation shrinkage," is a major factor that shortens the lifespan of structures.

Our research focused on understanding the mechanism of this shrinkage, specifically investigating how "water content" or "relative humidity (RH)" influences the process.

### Tracking the Transformation of C-A-S-H, Cement's Backbone

A primary component formed when cement hardens is "C-A-S-H," which stands for calcium aluminosilicate hydrate. When C-A-S-H reacts with CO2, it undergoes changes: calcium can be removed (decalcification), or the C-A-S-H itself can break down into other substances (decomposition).

We exposed hardened cement paste to CO2 under various humidity conditions. Using a range of analytical techniques, such as X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR), we closely monitored how C-A-S-H transforms during carbonation.

### The Humidity-Controlled Secrets of Shrinkage

Our findings revealed that relative humidity significantly impacts cement shrinkage. We observed that as humidity decreases, both the amount of calcium removed from C-A-S-H and the extent of C-A-S-H decomposition into "aluminosilicate gel" increase.

Crucially, the "decomposition" of C-A-S-H, leading to the formation of aluminosilicate gel, was identified as a major contributor to carbonation shrinkage. While previous theories often focused on calcium loss and water evaporation, this study highlights the significant role of C-A-S-H decomposition and the resulting structural changes in causing shrinkage.

### Paving the Way for More Durable Cement

This research represents a significant step towards a deeper understanding of carbonation shrinkage mechanisms in cement. By clarifying how humidity affects the internal structure of cement and subsequently leads to shrinkage, this work is expected to contribute to the development of more crack-resistant and longer-lasting cement materials in the future, enhancing the durability of our infrastructure.
